Practical Recommendation

Palladium is the top of Bilt's lineup, which launched in February 2026 alongside Blue ($0) and Obsidian ($95). It is a $495 metal World Legend Mastercard, and the honest way to describe it is that it is a premium travel card that happens to also do housing — not a rent card with premium perks bolted on.

The fee math is unusually clean for a card at this price. You get a $400 Bilt Travel hotel credit (two $200 halves, two-night minimum per booking) and $200 in Bilt Cash deposited each January. That is $600 against a $495 fee, before Priority Pass, before points, before the welcome bonus. The catch is the usual one: the hotel credit only works through Bilt's travel portal on stays of two nights or more, so if you only ever book direct or take one-night trips, half of that $600 is theoretical.

The earning is the other half of the case. 2x on literally everything is a strong default — it beats most flat-rate cards once you account for Bilt points transferring 1:1 to 23 airline and hotel partners. Partner restaurants go up to 5x, Bilt Travel hotels and Lyft hit 4x, and Bilt Travel flights hit 3x. Housing works the same as on the other two cards: pick Housing-Only Rewards for up to 1.25x on rent, mortgage, or HOA scaled to your everyday spend ratio (0.5x at 25%, 0.75x at 50%, 1x at 75%, 1.25x at 100%+), or pick Bilt Cash mode for 4% back that converts at $30 per 1,000 housing points.

Bottom line: if you fly a few times a year, will book two hotel stays through Bilt, and want one card you can put everything on at 2x, Palladium is a genuinely good deal and the credits do most of the work. If you would not use Priority Pass, will not book through the portal, or your card spending is light, this is $495 for a lounge card you do not need — take Obsidian at $95 or Blue at $0 and keep the difference. Note what is missing at this price: no Global Entry or TSA PreCheck credit, which nearly every other $400-plus card includes.

Practical Advice

This is what makes or breaks a card for you. If you can use these credits in your normal spend, the perks and protections are all bonus for you. We have a calculator at the bottom of this section to help you calculate that. Below we break down exactly what every credit is to show you if it's easy or if they're going to make you jump through 17 hoops to redeem it.

  • $400 Bilt Travel Hotel Credit – Up to $400 per calendar year toward hotel bookings through the Bilt Travel portal, delivered as two $200 statement credits (up to $200 January–June and up to $200 July–December). Every qualifying booking needs a two-night minimum stay. This is the biggest single line item on the card and the one that decides whether the $495 works. Price the portal against booking direct first: a $200 credit is real money, but portal bookings usually mean no hotel elite benefits, no stay credit, and sometimes a worse base rate. Miss a half of the year and that $200 is gone.
  • $200 Annual Bilt Cash – $200 in Bilt Cash lands when you open the account and again every January 1. Bilt Cash spends dollar-for-dollar at partner merchants (Lyft, hotels, fitness, groceries, pharmacy) or can be converted into housing points at $30 per 1,000 points. Limits & catches: it expires December 31 each year and only up to $100 rolls over, so treat it as a use-it-this-year benefit rather than a savings account.

Perks

  • Priority Pass membership (you plus 2 guests) – Full Priority Pass Select membership covering 1,300+ airport lounges worldwide, and it extends to authorized users on the account. Limits & catches: guests beyond the first two run about $35 each, and lounge availability is not guaranteed at busy times. Why care: this is the single perk that separates Palladium from Obsidian, and it is worth real money if you fly more than a couple of times a year.
  • Flat 2x on everything – Every non-housing purchase earns 2 points per dollar with no categories to track and no caps. Because Bilt points transfer 1:1 to 23 airline and hotel partners, that 2x is generally worth more than a 2% cash-back card if you redeem for travel.
  • Up to 5x at Bilt partner restaurants – Roughly 20,000+ participating U.S. restaurants stack a bonus on top of the 2x base. Limits & catches: it only applies at participating locations, which vary by city.
  • 4x hotels and 4x Lyft, 3x flights – Hotels booked through Bilt Travel and Lyft rides (with a linked account) hit 4x; flights booked through Bilt Travel hit 3x.
  • Rent, mortgage AND HOA, no transaction fee – Pay housing through Bilt and skip the usual 2–3% processing fee, with no annual points cap and support for multiple properties. Limits & catches: the payment is pulled from your bank by ACH rather than charged to the card, so it does not count as everyday spend.
  • Up to 1.25x on housing (Housing-Only Rewards mode) – Your housing multiplier scales with how much you charge elsewhere in the same billing cycle: about 0.5x at 25% of your housing payment, 0.75x at 50%, 1x at 75%, and the full 1.25x at 100% or more. Limits & catches: choosing this mode means giving up Bilt Cash earning.
  • Or 4% back in Bilt Cash (Bilt Cash mode) – Instead of the housing tiers, earn 4% back in Bilt Cash on everyday spend, where $30 of Bilt Cash converts into 1,000 points on a housing payment. Limits & catches: Bilt Cash expires December 31 and only up to $100 rolls over. You can switch modes, effective the next billing cycle.
  • Point Accelerators – Spend Bilt Cash to lift your base earning from 2x to 3x on the next $5,000 of everyday purchases, up to five times a year. Why care: a way to convert Bilt Cash into points if housing is not where you want it.
  • Bilt Gold status with the welcome bonus – Complete the $4,000 non-housing spend in 90 days and you get 50,000 points plus Bilt Gold elite status, which improves Bilt Rewards perks and partner offers.
  • Rent Day double points – On the 1st of each month, earn double on everyday purchases (1,000 bonus-point monthly cap), currently confirmed through January 1, 2027.
  • Point transfers (1:1) to 23 partners – Move points to United MileagePlus, Alaska Atmos Rewards, World of Hyatt and 20 others. Why care: this is where 2x on everything turns into outsized value.
  • No foreign transaction fees – Spend abroad without the ~3% penalty. Authorized users are $95 each and include Priority Pass.
  • What's missing – There is no Global Entry or TSA PreCheck application credit, which most cards in this fee range include. Factor that in if you were counting on it.

Protections

  • Cellular wireless telephone protection – Pay your monthly wireless bill with the card and get up to $800 per claim (with a per-year claim limit and deductible) for a damaged or stolen phone. Why care: can replace a separate phone insurance line item.
  • Trip cancellation and interruption – Reimburses non-refundable trip costs, generally up to $2,000 per trip, when a covered reason cancels or cuts short a trip charged to the card. Why care: the coverage that matters most when a trip falls apart.
  • Trip delay reimbursement – Covers reasonable meals and lodging, generally up to $300, when a covered trip is delayed long enough to trigger the benefit. Why care: an airline meltdown stops being your expense.
  • Rental car collision coverage – Decline the rental agency's damage waiver and pay with the card for coverage on theft or collision damage. Why care: saves the daily waiver fee on every rental.
  • Lost or damaged luggage protection – Coverage for checked and carry-on bags lost or damaged in transit. Why care: airlines rarely make you whole on their own.
  • Baggage delay insurance – Reimburses essentials when your bag does not arrive with you and the delay exceeds the benefit's threshold.
  • Purchase assurance – Covers eligible new purchases against theft or damage for a limited window after purchase, with per-claim and annual caps. Why care: useful on electronics and furniture.
  • Mastercard World Legend benefits – Palladium sits on Mastercard's top consumer tier, which carries a richer protections and concierge package than the World Elite benefits on Blue and Obsidian. Read the guide to benefits for exact limits before you rely on any single coverage.
