Marriott Boundless Credit Limit: What You Can Know Before Applying

Published September 19, 2026

By Carter

Looking for the Marriott Bonvoy Boundless card’s credit limit? Chase assigns your individual credit limit. We could not verify a Boundless-specific minimum or maximum in the official pages we could read; the linked Pricing & Terms disclosure was unavailable during this review. This does not establish that no minimum exists. Check the current application disclosures for the offer you are considering.

Need a particular credit limit to make a travel card work? Don't build your plans around a number the issuer hasn't assigned. A credit limit is borrowing capacity—not your travel budget.

What you can know before applying

Chase says credit limits are assigned by the lender, with factors including income, debt, payment history, account history, and current accounts. Those factors help explain the decision; they don't let us promise your individual limit.

Someone else's approval is not your spending plan. Treat a claimed minimum as unverified unless the specific card's official application terms establish it.

Chase's general explanation is available at https://www.chase.com/personal/credit-cards/education/basics/credit-card-limit. It explains how limits work; it does not establish a minimum for a particular card.

Credit limit and available credit are different

Your credit limit is the ceiling assigned to the account. Available credit is the portion left to use after borrowing against it.

Here's a hypothetical example, not a quoted Marriott Bonvoy Boundless approval. The $5,000 limit is an illustration, not a promised starting limit for any product.

Hypothetical available credit

Credit limit$5,000
Posted balance to subtract$1,200
Available credit before pending holds or other adjustments$3,800

The catch: that calculation describes room to borrow. It says nothing about how much you can comfortably repay in full. The bank has not approved your vacation budget.

Keep the spending requirement separate

A welcome offer's spending requirement is a target for qualifying purchases under that offer's terms. It is not a promise that your assigned credit limit will match the target, and it should not become a reason to spend beyond your budget.

Before applying, evaluate the hotel stays you would realistically book and the card's full terms. Any welcome offer should fit purchases you already intended to make and can afford to pay in full.

What to do with an existing account

Check your Chase account for your current credit limit and available credit. Your statement reflects an earlier snapshot. For account questions, call the number on the back of your card.

Practical verdict

Use this approach if you're deciding whether a travel card fits an existing budget. Skip applying just to discover a limit—or making a booking plan that depends on receiving a particular one. Set the amount you can repay first; let the credit limit remain a boundary, not a goal.
