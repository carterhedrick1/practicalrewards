Atmos Ascent vs. Summit: Are Your Trips Worth the Extra $300?

Published September 16, 2026

By Carter

Pay cash for trips with a companion? Atmos Ascent is the more natural starting point; Summit deserves a look when you regularly book award travel together and can use its broader benefits.

The decision is not simply which companion benefit sounds bigger. These cards solve different booking problems—and Summit costs $300 more annually.

Ascent: price the trip, not the discount

Ascent's companion fare starts at $122: the $99 fare plus taxes and fees from $23. That is the companion's minimum cost, not the total price for both travelers.

The cash companion fare covers eligible Saver or Main Cabin travel within North America. Book both travelers together on AlaskaAir.com and pay both fares with your eligible Atmos card. Redeem the code before its expiration.

Compare the complete companion-fare booking with the cheapest acceptable alternative for the same trip. A large discount from an expensive itinerary can still leave you paying more than you needed to. The discount does not get a trophy for participation.

The current offer requires $6,000 in purchases during the prior anniversary year for the recurring fare. Use normal spending to meet that requirement, and account for rewards you give up by moving purchases from another card.

For the trip calculation, use Practical Rewards' Alaska Companion Fare calculator, linked in the sources below. Keep trip savings separate from optional annual-fee allocation, and include cheaper alternative flights; this cash-fare approach does not apply to Summit's award certificates.

Summit: a points discount, not an unrestricted ticket

Summit's anniversary Global Companion Award discounts a companion's award ticket by up to 25,000 points when the primary cardholder travels with a companion, with both award tickets booked together on AlaskaAir.com. It works across Alaska, Hawaiian and their airline partners in any cabin, but the point limit still matters. One-way and round-trip bookings qualify; multi-city and open-jaw itineraries do not.

The catch: you need a bookable award itinerary for both travelers, enough points for the remaining cost, and cash for applicable taxes and fees paid with Summit. Start with a trip you would actually take—not a premium-cabin cash price you would never pay.

A separate 100,000-point Global Companion Award requires $60,000 in purchases during a card anniversary year. Leave it out of the decision unless that spending already fits your budget and card strategy.

What else would you actually use?

Both cards offer one free checked bag for the cardholder and up to six guests on the same reservation on qualifying Alaska or Hawaiian flights paid with the card. Those shared benefits can support keeping either card, but they do not justify paying extra for Summit.

Summit issues two Alaska Lounge passes each calendar quarter; unused passes expire that quarter. Each pass covers one adult plus up to two accompanying children under 21, so two adults need two passes. Summit also provides 10,000 anniversary status points and 3x points on eligible dining and foreign purchases. Count lounge visits that fit your travel schedule and status points that help a realistic status goal; neither needs a made-up dollar value.

The annual break-even test

For either card, add the companion savings you can realistically use, baggage savings and other benefits you would otherwise pay for. Subtract any extra booking costs and rewards sacrificed to meet spending requirements; that net value needs to cover Ascent's $95 fee or Summit's $395 fee.

For the upgrade decision, $395 minus $95 equals $300. Summit needs to deliver more than $300 in additional annual value over your actual Ascent package—including the cash companion benefit you would give up—not merely offer more perks on paper.

Practical verdict

Choose Ascent when paid companion trips fit your plans and its spending requirement fits your normal budget. Choose Summit when award trips together, useful lounge visits and the rest of its package clear the extra $300 hurdle.

Mostly fly alone, rarely check bags or dislike managing award bookings? Skip the companion-benefit sales pitch and judge only what you will use. Pick the card for the trips already on your calendar.
