Should You Add Points to a Marriott Free Night Award?

Published September 20, 2026

By Carter

Image illustration: a hypothetical 35,000-point certificate plus 10,000 owned points covers an eligible 45,000-point standard-room night. Hotels shown are illustrative.

Already holding a Marriott Free Night Award? Add points when they buy a stay you actually prefer enough to justify spending them—skip the top-up when the certificate alone does the job.

This is a decision about using an award already in your account. Whether to apply for or renew a credit card is a separate calculation.

What adding points actually does

Marriott allows you to combine a Free Night Award with up to 25,000 points. That expands the eligible redemption level; it does not turn the certificate into cash.

The award covers an eligible standard-room night, including the room rate and applicable taxes. You still need eligible award availability for your dates, and certain hotels charge resort fees—the word “free” has paperwork.

Would the better hotel earn the extra points?

Consider a hypothetical certificate capped at 35,000 points and two hypothetical hotels with eligible standard-room availability. Both fit the trip: Hotel A requires 35,000 points, so the certificate alone covers it; Hotel B requires 45,000 points.

Hypothetical Hotel B redemption

Certificate point cap35,000 points
Points added from your balance10,000 points
Eligible night covered45,000 points

The question is whether Hotel B's location, comfort, or convenience is worth 10,000 points to you, plus any difference in cash charges. A higher points price is not, by itself, an improvement.

Both choices consume the same certificate. The extra points are the incremental cost of choosing B—you are not spending 45,000 points from your balance, but you are not getting B without a tradeoff either.

Check the alternatives before booking

  • Compare the actual all-in cash price of somewhere you would willingly book. A hotel you would never pay for is a poor savings benchmark.
  • Consider what those extra points could realistically do for another trip. Use their value to you, not a guaranteed cash conversion.
  • Include any resort fees and other cash costs attached to the award stay.
  • Consider another realistic use of this same certificate before its expiration. Using it here means giving up that option.

If the certificate would otherwise go unused, a modest top-up can be sensible. If it already covers another planned stay, that alternative deserves a place in the comparison.

The catch: the calendar still matters

Check the expiration shown on your award and confirm that your stay qualifies before booking. Marriott's published terms say awards must be used on or before their expiry date and cannot be extended.

This example assumes you already own the extra points. Buying them introduces a cash purchase that needs its own comparison; it does not automatically make the redemption worthwhile.

Practical verdict

Add points when they make an existing certificate meaningfully more useful for a trip you intend to take. Skip the top-up when a certificate-only stay works just as well, or when the points and certificate have better realistic uses elsewhere. The allowance is a ceiling, not an assignment.
