How We Write

Credit card advice is full of polished sales copy and suspiciously perfect recommendations. Here is how our blog actually gets made, where the numbers come from, and what we do when something changes.

We use AI—a lot

Our posts run under Carter's byline and are produced with heavy AI assistance through an automated publishing pipeline he built. AI helps research, organize, draft, and format the work; the byline does not mean Carter personally wrote or reviewed each post. We are saying that plainly because readers should know how the article in front of them was made.

Automation is useful, but it is not a source. A confident sentence can still be wrong. That is why our pipeline checks facts against actual source material instead of trusting a draft because it sounds polished.

Card numbers get checked before publishing

Every card number in a post—fees, earning rates, credits, bonus details, and other terms—is checked against the same card database that powers our card pages before anything publishes. This gives the blog and the rest of Practical Rewards one shared set of card facts instead of several copies drifting out of sync.

Card terms can still change with little warning. A correct number today is not a promise that an issuer will keep it tomorrow, so our posts show when their facts were last verified.

We cite primary sources

When an issuer, airline, hotel program, regulator, or other organization publishes the original rule or announcement, we cite and link to that primary source. We may use other reporting to understand the story, but we want readers to be able to inspect the underlying terms for themselves.

No affiliate relationships

Practical Rewards has no affiliate relationships. No card issuer pays us, and nothing is ranked by payout. If we say a card is a poor fit, there is no commission waiting to persuade us otherwise.

Rumors stay rumors

Unconfirmed information is labeled [Rumor] until a primary source confirms it. A popular screenshot, a forum post, or a wave of identical social posts does not turn a rumor into a fact.

Corrections

If we get something wrong, please tell us through our contact page. We check the claim against the best available source, fix confirmed errors, and add a dated correction note so the change is visible rather than quietly rewriting history.
